## Portfolio redesign / token efficiency (Day 3)

> Redesigning My Portfolio—My passion project
>
> Day 3:
>
> Ok, so I've taken about 27 million detours while creating this project.
>
> As you may know, I decided to code my portfolio instead of sticking with Framer or other no-code tools.
>
> Not because I had to, but because this is my passion project.
>
> I want to figure out what the best product designer-friendly workflows actually are (for me, at last) and experiment with the new hyped AI updates to become proficient.
>
> And of course, learning carries its costs.
>
> As some of you pointed out, my token usage is probably high with Jacki.ai (AI agent I'm building for my portfolio) and all the heavy setup I've been doing.
>
> And yeah, it is. It makes sense.
>
> But instead of scaling everything back or stopping altogether, I just need to get better at being token-efficient.
>
> So I took a pause and started reading a ton about all sorts of designer workflows using AI, Claude token usage, etc.
>
> I found this guide that I found particularly helpful (kudos to Carmen Rincon for writing it!).
>
> It helped me understand many things I honestly didn't know:
>
> LLMs (or at least Claude, from what I've read so far) reprocess the entire conversation each time.
>
> So, as Carmen says in her guide "Message #30 costs as much as messages 1–30 combined."
>
> That kind of blew my mind.
>
> It also explains why it's so easy to burn through tokens without realizing it.
>
> For everyone... not just designers.
>
> Another takeaway that shifted how I'm working:
>
> Instead of pasting URLs or giving Claude large, messy inputs, use markdown so it's only reading clean text.
>
> Right now, I'm building a Claude skill for myself (and other designers) that:
> - Teaches better token usage
> - Evaluates my recent prompts
> - Helps me catch inefficiencies early
>
> Creating the skill itself isn't too complicated; I'd be done by now if I hadn't started writing this post. 😂
>
> More updates to come.
>
> Cheers,
>
> J

## TED AI 2025 — Herbst Theatre

> Dear Diary,
>
> Remind me never to say, "Yeah! We'll just tidy a few slides real quick."
>
> Because apparently, that's designer code for "let's accidentally stay up working way too late." 😂
>
> The night before TED AI 2025, three of us turned the Herbst Theatre into a caffeine-fueled design zone — tweaking slides, syncing fonts, and laughing our way through the chaos as the city went quiet around us.
>
> Nearing the end, one laptop died mid-slide (RIP), two were still standing, and we somehow pulled off the very (VERY) last-minute design change we'd been tasked with.
>
> Our spirits were still glowing, and we were too caffeinated to sleep but too sleepy for more coffee. 😂
>
> Honestly, we were just happy.
>
> The TED slides turned out great, and we came out with a few fun pics and videos of us in our impromptu co-dying session.
>
> By the end, we did the only reasonable thing: Waymo'd home — half-delirious, fully proud.
>
> It wasn't glamorous. It was teamwork, care, and pure fun.
>
> The kind of night that reminds you why you love design — and the people who stay late to make it better. ☕️💀✨
>
> TED is forever just so so cool.
>
> Thanks team Zhanna Tekleva, Mariia Velikdanova
>
> Cheers,
>
