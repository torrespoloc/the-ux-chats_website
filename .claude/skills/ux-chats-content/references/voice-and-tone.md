# The UX Chats — Brand Voice & Writing Rules

## Who we are

A free design community founded by Jacki during a job search, because she needed connection. ~170+ members on Luma/Discord. Runs game nights, design walks, guest speaker sessions, and craft nights. All free, all casual.

**Brand in one line:** A casual UX community where designers can be raw and real. No formal attire, no stiff intros.

**Design vibe (when referencing the brand visually):** Playful "sticker zine" — thick black outlines, hard offset shadows, rotated stickers, chunky Bricolage Grotesque headlines, warm cream paper. Never gradients, glassmorphism, or generic dark-SaaS.

---

## Brand Voice

- **Clear, warm, smart, design-savvy.** We know what we're talking about, but we never talk down or show off.
- **Conversational, not sloppy.** Write like you're texting a friend who happens to be a great editor — casual rhythm, clean execution.
- **Credible and modern, never corporate.** No "we are pleased to announce." No LinkedIn-brain phrasing.
- **Second person.** Talk TO people, not at them. "You've got opinions. You've failed (and learned). You keep it real."

### The anti-style — what we are NOT

- Not corporate/SaaS marketing speak ("we're thrilled to," "don't miss out")
- Not overly academic or formal
- Not desperate ("please join us!!", "spots filling fast!!")
- Not generic "design community" boilerplate — every post should sound like it could only be from us
- Not hypey. No exclamation-point stacking, no forced urgency
- Not AI-sounding. See the banned-phrase list below.

---

## Writing Rules

1. **Plain English.** If a sentence needs a second read, cut it.
2. **Short, punchy sentences.** Fragments are fine. One idea per line.
3. **Be specific, not vague.** "Three of us turned the Herbst Theatre into a caffeine-fueled design zone" beats "we had a great time working together."
4. **Cut filler.** No throat-clearing openers, no restating the obvious.
5. **Culturally aware, current.** References should feel like they're from this year, not a stock marketing calendar.
6. **Emoji: sparingly, as accents.** On-brand set: 👋 ✦ 🎮 🤔 🎨 ⚡. Never decoration spam (no emoji at the end of every line).
7. **No em dashes, ever.** Use a hyphen, a period, or start a new sentence.
8. **No bold text** in body copy — let the words carry the emphasis, not formatting.
9. **Real links only.** Never "DM me for the link" — put the actual Luma/Discord link directly in the copy.
10. **Parenthetical asides are our signature move** — humor, vulnerability, or a wink tucked in parens: "(RIP)", "(lol)", "(key)."

### Banned phrases (AI-sounding / marketing-hypey — never use)

"Don't miss out," "we're thrilled/excited to announce," "in today's fast-paced world," "unlock your potential," "game-changing," "elevate your," "dive into," "at the end of the day," "it's not just X, it's Y," "join us on this journey," "we can't wait to see you there!!," "spots are limited!!"

### Signature phrases (reuse, don't overuse)

"raw and real," "take off your tie," "no stiff intros," "Come as you are. Leave your LinkedIn voice at the door."

---

## Voice Mechanics (for LinkedIn / long-form specifically)

1. **Parenthetical asides** — the one-word parenthetical punch is a signature move.
2. **Exaggerated specificity for comic effect** — "27 million detours," not "a lot of detours." Not literal, makes the feeling land.
3. **Short punchy lines + sentence fragments** — a one-line paragraph for emphasis.
4. **Register shift** — casual to suddenly earnest mid-post: "It wasn't glamorous. It was teamwork, care, and pure fun."
5. **Self-deprecation as warmth** — undercut before anyone else can.
6. **Technical details framed as human problems** — feeling first, mechanism second.
7. **Occasional ALL CAPS for emphasis** — a vocal gesture, not shouting.

For short-form (reminders, blurbs, CTAs), rules 1-9 above still apply, but drop the long-form mechanics — those formats need to land in one or two lines, not build an arc.
