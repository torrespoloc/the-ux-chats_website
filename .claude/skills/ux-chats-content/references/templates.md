# The UX Chats — Content Templates

Voice rules for all of these live in `voice-and-tone.md` — read that first. Every template below assumes those rules are already applied.

---

## 1. Newsletter Intro

**Purpose:** The first 2-3 sentences of an email — decides whether someone keeps reading.

**Structure:**
1. **Open mid-thought or mid-scene.** Not "Hey everyone!" — start like you're already talking.
2. **One real, specific detail** from the community (a moment, a number, a quote) — not a generic recap.
3. **Bridge to what's in this issue** — one line, plain language, no "in this issue we cover..."

**Length:** 40-80 words. This is a doorway, not the room.

**Never:** "Hope this email finds you well," "Welcome to another edition of...," a bullet list of contents before any voice has been established.

---

## 2. Event Announcement

**Goal:** FOMO + crystal-clear format. People skip events when they don't understand what they're signing up for.

**Structure:**
1. **Hook** — a specific funny/memorable moment from a past edition, a curious question, or just state what's happening with energy.
2. **What it is** — plain language. What do people actually DO at this thing?
3. **Why it's worth showing up** — real value, not "great networking opportunity."
4. **Details block** — day, date, time, timezone, price, the real Luma link directly.
5. **Close** — short, warm. "Hope to see you there."

**Length:** 100-180 words for social; can run longer for email/newsletter placement.

## 3. LinkedIn Post

Three sub-formats, pick based on the moment:

### Event Recap (after an event)
1. Hook — the most absurd/funny/memorable moment.
2. What happened — brief format explanation for people who weren't there.
3. What made it special — something real and specific.
4. What's next — next event, how to join.

### Hot Take UX Post (game-specific)
1. Hook — "Imagine defending something ridiculous in front of a room of designers..."
2. Explain the game mechanics.
3. What makes it fun — confidence, improv, quick thinking.
4. Call to join the next one.

### Community Update
1. Hook — exciting news or milestone.
2. Backstory — how we got here.
3. What's happening now.
4. What's next.
5. Invitation to participate.

**Mechanics:** 150-250 words (1,200-2,000 characters) is the engagement sweet spot. Links go in the first comment, not the body (25-68% reach penalty for in-body links). Max 3 hashtags. Post Tue-Thu, 8-10am or 12-1pm PT.

## 4. Guest Spotlight

**Purpose:** Announce or thank a guest speaker — makes them look good, makes the event look worth attending.

**Structure:**
1. **Hook** — lead with what makes THIS person's perspective worth hearing, not their title.
2. **Who they are** — one or two sentences, plain language, no resume-speak.
3. **What they'll cover / what they brought** — specific, not "insights into design."
4. **The human detail** — something that makes them feel like a real person, not a keynote slide (a quote, a quirk, an aside).
5. **CTA** — when/where to catch the session, or how to say thanks if it already happened.

**Never:** Turning their bio into a LinkedIn resume dump. Every credential you list should earn its place by making the audience want to show up.

---

## 5. Call to Action (standalone)

**Purpose:** A single, direct ask — join Discord, RSVP, apply to speak, fill out a form.

**Structure:**
1. **The ask, stated plainly, first.** Don't bury it under three sentences of context.
2. **One line of "why now" or "why this matters to you"** — not why it matters to us.
3. **The real link.**

**Length:** 20-50 words. If it's longer than that, it's not a CTA, it's an announcement — use that template instead.

---

## 6. Follow-Up Reminder

**Purpose:** "This is happening soon" — for people who already know about the event but haven't RSVP'd or forgot the details.

**Structure:**
1. **Lead with time pressure, stated plainly** — "Tomorrow night," "In 3 hours" — not "Just a friendly reminder!"
2. **One-line refresher** on what it is (for people who forgot, not people who need convincing).
3. **The details + link again.** Never assume they remember it from last time.

**Length:** 20-40 words. Reminders are utilities, not pitches — resist the urge to re-sell the event here.

**Never:** "Just a friendly reminder!," "Don't forget!," "Last chance!!" — state the fact, trust the reader.

---

## 7. Short Product/Update Blurb

**Purpose:** A quick community update — a new feature, a small milestone, a behind-the-scenes note. Not a full LinkedIn post, not a newsletter section.

**Structure:**
1. **The update, stated plainly, first sentence.**
2. **One line of why it matters or how it happened** (optional — only if it adds something real).

**Length:** 15-40 words. This is a Slack-message length update, not a story.

## Format Quick-Reference

| Format | Length | Link placement | Emoji | Tone |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Newsletter intro | 40-80 words | inline ok | sparing | warm, scene-setting |
| Event announcement | 100-180 words | in details block | 1-2 | energetic, clear |
| LinkedIn post | 150-250 words | first comment | 1-3 | full voice, arc |
| Guest spotlight | 80-150 words | in CTA | sparing | warm, specific |
| Call to action | 20-50 words | inline, direct | 0-1 | direct, plain |
| Follow-up reminder | 20-40 words | inline, direct | 0-1 | plain, utility |
| Short blurb | 15-40 words | optional | 0-1 | plain, quick |
